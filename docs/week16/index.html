Week 16: APA Report Writing & Research Project

PS50008A Research Methods | 23-27 February 2026

Author

Gordon Wright

Published

23 February 2026

Overview

ImportantResearch Plan Due This Week

Your Research Plan is due Friday 27 February 2026, 12 noon. Submit via Learn. This is your first assessed piece of coursework for the module.

This week: Learn how to structure an APA-style research report and how to write a Method section (using your Research Plan as a worked example). We also launch the group research project — a separate study from your Research Plan — which will become your Research Report (due mid-late April).

Lecture: Wednesday 10:00-12:00 RHBD 143 (2 hours) Lab: Thursday 10:00-12:00 WB304A

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

APA Report Structure

  • Identify the sections of an APA-style lab report (Title, Abstract, Introduction, Method, Results, Discussion, References)
  • Understand the purpose and content of each section
  • Recognise how sections connect to tell a coherent research story

Writing a Method Section

  • See how a Method section is structured (using your Research Plan as a worked example)
  • Understand what makes a good Design, Participants, Materials, and Procedure subsection
  • Identify common mistakes in method writing and how to avoid them

Writing for Psychology

  • Use appropriate academic tone and APA conventions
  • Report statistical information correctly (notation, formatting)
  • Structure paragraphs effectively in scientific writing

Looking Ahead to the Research Report

  • Understand the full timeline from Research Plan to Research Report
  • See how group project work feeds into an individual write-up
  • Plan your approach to the Research Report (due mid-late April)

Before the Lecture

ImportantRequired Preparation

  2. Read the Research Plan assessment brief — make sure you understand what’s required
  3. Look at the Research Report assessment brief — see where this is all heading

The Big Picture

This week answers the question: How do psychologists communicate their research?

TipYour Research Plan Practises Method Writing

Your Research Plan and your Research Report are separate assessments on different topics. But the Research Plan is structured exactly like a Method section — the same skills apply when you write your Research Report later. Think of it as a rehearsal.

The lecture walks through the full anatomy of an APA lab report, using your Research Plan as a worked example of Method section writing. We also introduce the group research project, which is a different study that will become your Research Report.

Key Concepts

The APA Lab Report

Section What it does When you’ll write it
Title Page Informative title, your name, student number Week 20 onwards
Abstract ~150-word summary of the whole study Write last
Introduction Background literature, rationale, hypothesis Weeks 17-18
Method Design, Participants, Materials, Procedure This week (= your Research Plan)
Results Descriptive and inferential statistics Week 19-20
Discussion Interpretation, limitations, future directions Week 20 onwards
References APA-formatted reference list Throughout

Your Research Plan → Your Method Section

Research Plan component Method subsection
Your hypothesis (one-tailed/two-tailed) Stated at end of Introduction (and referenced in Design)
Design choice and justification Design — between/within, IV, DV
Who you would test and how many Participants — sample, recruitment, demographics
What you would need Materials — stimuli, measures, equipment
Step-by-step what happens Procedure — chronological account of what participants experience
Statistical test selection Referenced in Design, detailed in Results

APA Writing Conventions

  • Past tense for Method and Results (describing what was done)
  • Present tense for established findings in Introduction
  • Third person — avoid “I” and “we” where possible
  • Statistical notation — italicise test statistics (t, r, F, p)
  • Exact p-values — report to three decimal places (e.g., p = .023)
