Week 17: MCQ & EDS Preparation

PS50008A Research Methods | 2-6 March 2026

Author

Gordon Wright

Published

2 March 2026

Overview

WarningAssessments This Fortnight
  • MCQ Exam: Wednesday 11 March — 36 questions (5 investigations + 6 general)
  • EDS Assignment (Experimental Design & Statistics): Due 20 March — interpret R output, answer methods questions (100 marks)

Lecture (Wednesday): Focused on Experimental Design & Statistics (EDS) and MCQ preparation — everything you need for both assessments.

Lab (Thursday): Project Toolkit — Phase 1. You work through the Project Toolkit: draft core documents (Participant Information Sheet, Consent Form, Debrief Form, Participant Instructions) and start the Researcher Procedure. These labs (17 and 18) complete the toolkit so you are ready for data collection in Week 19.

Lecture: Wednesday 10:00-12:00 RHBD 143
Lab: Thursday 10:00-12:00 WB304A

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

For the MCQ Exam

  • Identify design type (between-participants, within-participants, correlational) from a study description
  • State the independent and dependent variable (or recognise “no IV” in correlational designs)
  • Distinguish one-tailed and two-tailed hypotheses and match them to scenarios
  • Choose the correct statistical test (independent-samples t-test, paired-samples t-test, Pearson’s r) for each design
  • Interpret p-values and significance (p < .05, p > .05, p < .01)
  • Explain correlation (positive/negative) and why correlation does not imply causation
  • Define operationalisation, replication, confounding variable, null hypothesis, population

For the EDS Assignment

  • State a clear experimental hypothesis (directional or non-directional) with IV and DV
  • Identify design type, IV (with levels), DV, and type of data (ratio/interval)
  • Report descriptive statistics in APA format (M, SD) and interpret the pattern
  • Justify use of paired vs independent t-test; report t, df, p; interpret significance at α = .05
  • Interpret a 95% confidence interval and relate it to significance
  • Write a full APA-style results paragraph (descriptives + inferential + conclusion)
  • Identify outliers, explain their effect on SD, and discuss reporting with/without (sensitivity)
  • Suggest methodological improvements with justification
  • Discuss individual differences (e.g. heavy vs light users, age) and ecological validity

The Big Picture

Week 17 ties together design, analysis, and interpretation so you can perform under exam conditions and complete the EDS paper. Both assessments reward precise use of terminology and clear reasoning.

TipConnecting the Two Assessments

The MCQ tests recognition and application (e.g. “What design?” “What test?”). The EDS (Experimental Design & Statistics) assignment tests deeper interpretation (e.g. “Why is a paired t-test appropriate?” “What does this CI tell us?”). The same concepts underpin both.

Key Concepts at a Glance

Design → Test (MCQ)

Design IV/DV Typical test
Between-participants One IV (2 levels), one DV Independent-samples t-test
Within-participants (repeated measures) One IV (2 levels), one DV Paired-samples t-test
Correlational No IV; two measured variables Pearson’s r

EDS Section A (Mechanics)

  • Hypothesis: Clear statement with IV and DV (directional or non-directional).
  • Design/IV/DV/Data: Repeated measures; IV with levels; DV; ratio/interval data.
  • APA descriptives: M and SD per condition; interpret pattern.
  • Paired t-test: Why paired; report t, df, p; significant at α = .05?; interpret 95% CI.
  • Results paragraph: Test name, descriptives, statistic, conclusion.
  • Data quality: Identify outlier; reason; effect on SD; report both analyses.

EDS Section B (Theory)

  • Improvements: Two methodological changes with clear justification.
  • Individual differences: e.g. heavy vs light users, younger vs older — reasoned discussion.
  • Ecological validity: New study with hypothesis, IV (levels), DV, procedure.
