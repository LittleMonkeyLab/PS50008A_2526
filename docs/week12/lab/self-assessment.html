Distributions & Variability Practice

Overview

This self-assessment consolidates your learning from Week 12. Complete these exercises before next Monday’s lecture.

NoteFormative Assessment

This self-assessment is not graded but completing it will prepare you for: - The MCQ Exam (identifying distributions and statistics) - The EDS Assignment (calculating by hand) - The Research Report (reporting descriptives)

Part 1: Calculating by Hand

For the EDS Assignment, you’ll need to calculate statistics by hand. Practice now!

Exercise 1: Mean and Median

A researcher measured the number of words recalled by 8 participants:

Data: 5, 7, 6, 8, 6, 9, 6, 5

a) Calculate the mean.

TipShow Answer

Sum = 5 + 7 + 6 + 8 + 6 + 9 + 6 + 5 = 52

Mean = 52 / 8 = 6.5 words

b) Calculate the median.

TipShow Answer

Sorted data: 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 8, 9

Middle positions: 4th and 5th values (since n = 8)

4th value = 6, 5th value = 6

Median = (6 + 6) / 2 = 6 words

c) What does the comparison of mean and median suggest about the distribution?

TipShow Answer

Mean (6.5) is slightly higher than median (6), suggesting a very slight positive skew. However, they’re close enough that the distribution is approximately symmetric.

Exercise 2: Standard Deviation

Using the same data: 5, 7, 6, 8, 6, 9, 6, 5 (Mean = 6.5)

Complete the variance calculation:

Value Deviation (x - mean) Squared
5 ? ?

6 ? ?

Sum ?
TipShow Answer
Value Deviation Squared
5 -1.5 2.25

Variance = 14.00 / (8-1) = 14 / 7 = 2.0

Part 2: Interpreting Distributions

Exercise 3: Distribution Shapes

For each scenario, predict the distribution shape:

a) Exam scores on an easy test (most students score 80-100%)

TipShow Answer

Negatively skewed (ceiling effect). Most scores cluster near the top, with a tail toward lower scores.

b) Reaction times to a visual stimulus

TipShow Answer

Positively skewed. There’s a lower bound (can’t be negative or below ~150ms), but no upper bound. Occasional slow responses create a right tail.

c) Heights of adult women

TipShow Answer

Approximately normal (symmetric, bell-shaped). Heights tend to cluster around the average with fewer very short or very tall individuals.

Exercise 4: Mean vs Median

A researcher reports: Mean salary = £52,000; Median salary = £38,000

a) What does this tell you about the distribution?

TipShow Answer

The mean is much higher than the median, indicating positive skew. A few very high salaries pull the mean up.

b) Which measure (mean or median) better represents the “typical” salary? Why?

TipShow Answer

The median (£38,000) better represents the typical salary because it’s not affected by the extreme high values. More than half of workers earn less than the mean.

Part 3: APA Reporting

Exercise 5: Write the Results

A study compared reaction times between caffeine and placebo conditions:

Group Mean RT (ms) SD
Caffeine 285 42
Placebo 312 51

Write a sentence reporting these results in APA format.

TipShow Answer

Participants in the caffeine condition responded faster (M = 285ms, SD = 42ms) than those in the placebo condition (M = 312ms, SD = 51ms).

Exercise 6: Complete the Report

Fill in the blanks using these statistics: - Memory task: Mean = 6.8 words, SD = 1.4 words, Median = 7 words

Participants recalled an average of _____ words (M = _____, SD = _____). The median was _____, suggesting the distribution was approximately _____.

TipShow Answer

Participants recalled an average of 6.8 words (M = 6.8, SD = 1.4). The median was 7, suggesting the distribution was approximately symmetric (since mean ≈ median).

Part 4: Conceptual Questions

Exercise 7: True or False

Mark each statement True or False:

a) The mean is always the best measure of central tendency.

TipShow Answer

False. The median is better for skewed data or when outliers are present.

b) Standard deviation tells you about the shape of the distribution.

TipShow Answer

False. SD tells you about spread/variability, not shape. You need to compare mean and median (or look at a histogram) to assess shape.

c) If the mean is greater than the median, the distribution is positively skewed.

TipShow Answer

True. In a positively skewed distribution, extreme high values pull the mean above the median.

d) Variance is always larger than standard deviation.

TipShow Answer

False. When variance < 1, taking the square root gives a larger SD. For example: Variance = 0.25, SD = 0.5. (However, for most psychological data with variance > 1, this statement would be true.)

Exercise 8: The 68-95-99.7 Rule

Reaction times are normally distributed with Mean = 300ms and SD = 40ms.

a) What range contains approximately 68% of reaction times?

TipShow Answer

Mean ± 1 SD = 300 ± 40 = 260ms to 340ms

b) What range contains approximately 95% of reaction times?

TipShow Answer

Mean ± 2 SD = 300 ± 80 = 220ms to 380ms

c) A participant has a reaction time of 200ms. Is this unusual? Why?

TipShow Answer

Yes, this is unusual. 200ms is (300-200)/40 = 2.5 SDs below the mean. Only about 0.6% of values fall this far from the mean in a normal distribution.

Summary

After completing this self-assessment, you should be able to:

  • Calculate mean, median, and SD by hand
  • Predict and identify distribution shapes
  • Report descriptives in APA format
  • Apply the 68-95-99.7 rule

  • Why might our sample mean differ from the true population mean?
