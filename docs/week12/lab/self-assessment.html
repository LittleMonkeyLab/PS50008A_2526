Week 12 Self-Assessment

Introduction to Reading R Output

Author

Gordon Wright

Published

23 January 2026

Overview

This self-assessment consolidates your learning from Week 12 and introduces you to reading R output - a key skill for the EDS Assignment.

NoteFormative Assessment

This self-assessment is not graded but completing it will prepare you for: - The MCQ Exam (identifying distributions and statistics) - The EDS Assignment (interpreting R output) - The Research Report (reporting descriptives)

Part 1: Reading R Descriptive Output

For the EDS Assignment, you’ll receive R output and need to interpret it. Let’s practice!

Exercise 1: Basic Descriptive Output

A researcher measured the number of words recalled by 8 participants. Here’s the R output:

> summary_stats <- data %>%
+   summarise(
+     Mean = mean(words_recalled),
+     SD = sd(words_recalled),
+     Median = median(words_recalled),
+     n = n()
+   )
> summary_stats

# A tibble: 1 x 4
   Mean    SD Median     n
  <dbl> <dbl>  <dbl> <int>
1   6.5  1.41      6     8

a) What is the mean number of words recalled?

TipShow Answer

The mean is 6.5 words (shown in the Mean column).

b) What is the standard deviation?

TipShow Answer

The standard deviation is 1.41 words (shown in the SD column).

c) What does the comparison of mean (6.5) and median (6) suggest about the distribution?

TipShow Answer

The mean (6.5) is slightly higher than the median (6), suggesting a very slight positive skew. However, they’re close enough that the distribution is approximately symmetric.

Exercise 2: Group Comparison Output

A study compared reaction times between two conditions. Here’s the R output:

> data %>%
+   group_by(condition) %>%
+   summarise(Mean = mean(RT), SD = sd(RT), n = n())

# A tibble: 2 x 4
  condition  Mean    SD     n
  <chr>     <dbl> <dbl> <int>
1 caffeine   285.  42.3    15
2 placebo    312.  51.1    15

a) How many participants were in each condition?

TipShow Answer

15 participants in each condition (shown in the n column).

b) Which condition had faster reaction times?

TipShow Answer

The caffeine condition had faster reaction times (lower mean: 285ms vs 312ms).

c) Write a sentence describing these results in APA format.

TipShow Answer

Participants in the caffeine condition responded faster (M = 285ms, SD = 42.3ms) than those in the placebo condition (M = 312ms, SD = 51.1ms).

Part 2: Interpreting Distributions

Exercise 3: Distribution Shapes

For each scenario, predict the distribution shape:

a) Exam scores on an easy test (most students score 80-100%)

TipShow Answer

Negatively skewed (ceiling effect). Most scores cluster near the top, with a tail toward lower scores.

b) Reaction times to a visual stimulus

TipShow Answer

Positively skewed. There’s a lower bound (can’t be negative or below ~150ms), but no upper bound. Occasional slow responses create a right tail.

c) Heights of adult women

TipShow Answer

Approximately normal (symmetric, bell-shaped). Heights tend to cluster around the average with fewer very short or very tall individuals.

Exercise 4: Mean vs Median in R Output

A researcher reports this R output:

> salary_stats
# A tibble: 1 x 2
     Mean Median
    <dbl>  <dbl>
1  52000  38000

a) What does this tell you about the distribution?

TipShow Answer

The mean (£52,000) is much higher than the median (£38,000), indicating positive skew. A few very high salaries pull the mean up.

b) Which measure (mean or median) better represents the “typical” salary? Why?

TipShow Answer

The median (£38,000) better represents the typical salary because it’s not affected by the extreme high values. More than half of workers earn less than the mean.

Part 3: APA Reporting from R Output

Exercise 5: Complete the Report

Use this R output to answer the questions:

> memory_stats
# A tibble: 1 x 3
   Mean    SD Median
  <dbl> <dbl>  <dbl>
1   6.8   1.4      7

Fill in the blanks:

Participants recalled an average of _____ words (M = _____, SD = _____). The median was _____, suggesting the distribution was approximately _____.

TipShow Answer

Participants recalled an average of 6.8 words (M = 6.8, SD = 1.4). The median was 7, suggesting the distribution was approximately symmetric (since mean ≈ median).

Exercise 6: Write Full APA Description

Use this R output:

> data %>%
+   group_by(time_of_day) %>%
+   summarise(Mean = mean(accuracy), SD = sd(accuracy), n = n())

# A tibble: 2 x 4
  time_of_day  Mean    SD     n
  <chr>       <dbl> <dbl> <int>
1 morning      78.2  12.4    20
2 evening      71.5  14.1    20

Write a complete sentence reporting these descriptives in APA format.

TipShow Answer

Participants showed higher accuracy in the morning (M = 78.2%, SD = 12.4%) compared to the evening (M = 71.5%, SD = 14.1%).

Or:

Mean accuracy was higher in the morning condition (M = 78.2, SD = 12.4) than in the evening condition (M = 71.5, SD = 14.1).

Part 4: Conceptual Questions

Exercise 7: True or False

Mark each statement True or False:

a) The mean is always the best measure of central tendency.

TipShow Answer

False. The median is better for skewed data or when outliers are present.

b) Standard deviation tells you about the shape of the distribution.

TipShow Answer

False. SD tells you about spread/variability, not shape. You need to compare mean and median (or look at a histogram) to assess shape.

c) If the mean is greater than the median, the distribution is positively skewed.

TipShow Answer

True. In a positively skewed distribution, extreme high values pull the mean above the median.

d) In R output, n typically refers to the sample size.

TipShow Answer

True. The n column shows the number of observations (participants/data points) in each group.

Exercise 8: The 68-95-99.7 Rule

Reaction times are normally distributed with Mean = 300ms and SD = 40ms.

a) What range contains approximately 68% of reaction times?

TipShow Answer

Mean ± 1 SD = 300 ± 40 = 260ms to 340ms

b) What range contains approximately 95% of reaction times?

TipShow Answer

Mean ± 2 SD = 300 ± 80 = 220ms to 380ms

c) A participant has a reaction time of 200ms. Is this unusual? Why?

TipShow Answer

Yes, this is unusual. 200ms is (300-200)/40 = 2.5 SDs below the mean. Only about 0.6% of values fall this far from the mean in a normal distribution.

Summary

After completing this self-assessment, you should be able to:

  • Read and interpret R descriptive output
  • Extract key statistics (Mean, SD, Median, n) from R tables
  • Predict and identify distribution shapes
  • Report descriptives in APA format
  • Understand the relationship between mean and median for skewness

  • Correlation coefficients
  • Scatterplots generated by R
  • Statistical significance of correlations
