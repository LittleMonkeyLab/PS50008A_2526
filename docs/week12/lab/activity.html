Summary

ImportantKey Takeaways

Design Types:

  • Between-subjects = Different people in each condition (Lab A vs Lab B)
  • Within-subjects = Same people in all conditions (Pre vs Post mood)
  • Correlational = No manipulation, measuring relationships (Self-rating vs accuracy)

Variables:

  • IV = What the researcher manipulates (word type, time)
  • DV = What the researcher measures (recall, mood rating)
  • Correlational designs have NO IV

Descriptive Statistics:

  • Mean = Average value
  • SD = How spread out values are
  • A difference between means matters if it’s big relative to the SD

There was a **hidden manipulation** in the memory task:

- **Lab A** memorised **abstract** words: justice, freedom, wisdom...

```{r}
#| label: lab-comparison
#| fig-height: 5
#| fig-align: center
memory_labs <- memory |>
  filter(!is.na(Q11) & !is.na(mem_total_1)) |>
  mutate(Lab = factor(Q11, levels = c(1, 2), labels = c("Lab A\n(Abstract)", "Lab B\n(Concrete)")))

lab_stats <- memory_labs |>
  group_by(Lab) |>
  summarise(
```

### Investigation 1: Throwing Accuracy

Students threw balls with their dominant hand (10 throws) and their non-dominant hand (5 throws). We measured accuracy (% successful throws).

**1. Design type:**

`r mcq(c("Between-subjects", answer = "Within-subjects", "Correlational"))`

**2. Why is this within-subjects?**

`r mcq(c("Different people used different hands", answer = "The same person threw with BOTH hands", "Hands were randomly assigned"))`

**3. What is the IV?**

`r mcq(c(answer = "Hand used (dominant vs non-dominant)", "Throwing accuracy", "Number of throws", "No IV"))`

**4. What is the DV?**

`r mcq(c("Hand used", answer = "Throwing accuracy (%)", "Number of throws", "Participant age"))`
:::

::: {.webex-check .webex-box}

### Investigation 2: Self-Rating vs Performance

Before throwing, students rated how good they thought they'd be (1-10). We then measured their actual accuracy.

**1. Design type:**

`r mcq(c("Between-subjects", "Within-subjects", answer = "Correlational"))`

**2. Is there an IV?**

`r mcq(c("Yes - self-rating", "Yes - actual accuracy", answer = "No - neither variable was manipulated"))`

**3. Why can't we say self-rating CAUSES good performance?**

`r mcq(c("Sample too small", "Wrong test", answer = "No manipulation means no causal claim"))`
:::

::: {.webex-check .webex-box}

### Investigation 3: Sixth Sense

Students tried to detect if someone was staring at them (6 trials). If just guessing, you'd expect 3/6 (50%) correct.

**1. If someone got 5/6 correct, does that PROVE they have a sixth sense?**

`r torf(FALSE)`

**2. Why not?**

`r mcq(c("5/6 is impossible", answer = "Some people will get 5/6 just by luck", "The experiment was flawed", "Sixth sense doesn't exist"))`

**3. What would 50% accuracy suggest?**

`r mcq(c("They definitely have a sixth sense", "They definitely don't have a sixth sense", answer = "Performance at chance level - just guessing"))`
:::

## Summary

::: {.callout-important}
## Key Takeaways

**Design Types:**

- **Between-subjects** = Different people in each condition (Lab A vs Lab B)
- **Within-subjects** = Same people in all conditions (Pre vs Post mood)
- **Correlational** = No manipulation, measuring relationships (Self-rating vs accuracy)

**Variables:**

- **IV** = What the researcher manipulates (word type, time)
- **DV** = What the researcher measures (recall, mood rating)
- Correlational designs have **NO IV**

**Descriptive Statistics:**

- **Mean** = Average value
- **SD** = How spread out values are
- A difference between means matters if it's **big relative to the SD**
:::
