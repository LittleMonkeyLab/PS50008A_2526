Week 13: Building Your Research

PS50008A Research Methods | 26-30 January 2026

Author

Gordon Wright

Published

26 January 2026

Overview

NoteFrom Learning to Doing

Last week you learned the framework for experimental research. This week we put it into practice — building the foundation for YOUR Research Plan.

This week: Learn to operationalise variables, write testable hypotheses, choose appropriate designs, and connect your design choices to statistical tests. This directly prepares you for the Research Plan (due Week 16).

Lecture: Wednesday 10:00-12:00 RHBD 143 (2 hours) Lab: Thursday 10:00-12:00 WB304A and WB304B

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

Operationalisation

  • Turn abstract concepts into measurable variables
  • Identify precise IV manipulations and DV measurements
  • Recognise common operationalisation pitfalls

Writing Hypotheses

  • Write clear null and alternative hypotheses
  • Choose between one-tailed and two-tailed hypotheses with justification
  • Understand when directional predictions are appropriate

Design Decisions

  • Choose between within-subjects and between-subjects designs
  • Explain the trade-offs for each design type
  • Apply practical constraints (time, participants, ethics) to design choices

Connecting Design to Analysis

  • Match your research design to the appropriate statistical test
  • Explain WHY different designs require different tests
  • Justify your choice of analysis method (required for your Research Plan)

Before the Lecture

ImportantRequired Preparation
  1. Review Week 12 content — hypotheses, IVs, DVs, and experimental designs
  2. Look at the Research Plan brief — understand what you need to produce

The Three Research Plan Topics

Your Research Plan will investigate one of these questions:

TipTopic 1: Font Difficulty → Memory

Does reading text in a harder-to-read font improve memory retention?

Based on research by Diemand-Yauman et al. (2011) on “desirable difficulties”

TipTopic 2: Hydration → Cognitive Performance

Does drinking water improve cognitive task performance?

Based on research by Edmonds & Burford (2009) on hydration and cognition

TipTopic 3: Eye Contact → Perceived Trustworthiness

Does eye contact duration affect how trustworthy a person is perceived to be?

Based on research by Argyle & Dean (1965) on eye contact and social perception

Key Concepts

Operationalisation

  • Definition: Turning abstract concepts into concrete, measurable variables
  • IV operationalisation: How exactly will you manipulate the independent variable?
  • DV operationalisation: How exactly will you measure the dependent variable?
  • Key question: Would another researcher be able to replicate your study from your description?

Hypotheses

  • Null hypothesis (H₀): There is no effect — the default assumption
  • Alternative hypothesis (H₁): There IS an effect — what you’re testing
  • One-tailed: Predicts direction (“A > B”) — use when theory/evidence strongly supports a direction
  • Two-tailed: Predicts difference without direction (“A ≠ B”) — use when direction is uncertain

Design Trade-offs

Design Advantage Disadvantage
Between-subjects No order effects, no practice effects Individual differences add noise, needs more participants
Within-subjects Controls individual differences, more statistical power Order effects possible, demand characteristics

The Design → Test Link

Your Design Your Statistical Test
Between-subjects (different people) Independent samples t-test
Within-subjects (same people) Paired samples t-test

This connection is ESSENTIAL for your Research Plan. You must justify why your design choice leads to your test choice.

## Key Concepts

### Operationalisation
- **Definition**: Turning abstract concepts into concrete, measurable variables
- **IV operationalisation**: How exactly will you manipulate the independent variable?
- **DV operationalisation**: How exactly will you measure the dependent variable?
- **Key question**: Would another researcher be able to replicate your study from your description?

### Hypotheses
- **Null hypothesis (H₀)**: There is no effect — the default assumption
- **Alternative hypothesis (H₁)**: There IS an effect — what you're testing
- **One-tailed**: Predicts direction ("A > B") — use when theory/evidence strongly supports a direction
- **Two-tailed**: Predicts difference without direction ("A ≠ B") — use when direction is uncertain

### Design Trade-offs
| Design | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| **Between-subjects** | No order effects, no practice effects | Individual differences add noise, needs more participants |
| **Within-subjects** | Controls individual differences, more statistical power | Order effects possible, demand characteristics |

### The Design → Test Link
| Your Design | Your Statistical Test |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Between-subjects (different people) | Independent samples t-test |
| Within-subjects (same people) | Paired samples t-test |

This connection is ESSENTIAL for your Research Plan. You must justify why your design choice leads to your test choice.
