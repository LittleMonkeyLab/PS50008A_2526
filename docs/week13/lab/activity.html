Understanding Operationalisation

TipWhat is Operationalisation?

Turning an abstract concept into a concrete, measurable variable. Your description must be specific enough that another researcher could replicate your study exactly.

1. Which of these is a well-operationalised IV?

2. Which of these is a well-operationalised DV?

Learning from Examples

Here’s how operationalisation works with topics different from your assessment:

Example: Caffeine → Alertness

IV: Caffeine consumption with two levels:

  • Condition 1: 200mg caffeine capsule (equivalent to ~2 cups of coffee)
  • Condition 2: Placebo capsule (identical appearance, no caffeine)

DV: Alertness measured by:

  • Task: Psychomotor Vigilance Test (10-minute sustained attention task)
  • Scoring: Mean reaction time to stimuli (milliseconds)
  • Range: Continuous, typically 200-500ms

Notice the specificity: exact dosage, how conditions are matched, what task, what metric.

IV: Audio condition with two levels:

  • Condition 1: Instrumental background music (60-70 dB, classical piano)

  • Task: Proofreading task — identify spelling errors in a 500-word passage
  • Scoring: Number of errors correctly identified
  • Range: 0-15 (15 errors embedded)

### One-Tailed vs Two-Tailed

**3. A one-tailed hypothesis:**

`r mcq(c("Tests for any difference", answer = "Predicts a specific direction of effect", "Is always more appropriate", "Requires no justification"))`

**4. A two-tailed hypothesis:**

`r mcq(c(answer = "Predicts a difference without specifying direction", "Predicts no effect", "Is never appropriate in psychology", "Is only used for correlations"))`

### Making the Decision

**5. When should you use a one-tailed hypothesis?**

`r mcq(c("Whenever you want more statistical power", "When you're not sure what will happen", answer = "When prior research consistently supports a specific direction", "Always - it's better practice"))`

**Example justification for direction:**

> "I have chosen a **one-tailed** hypothesis predicting that caffeine will **decrease** reaction times because:
>
> - McLellan et al. (2016) meta-analysis found caffeine consistently improved reaction time across 21 studies
> - The adenosine-blocking mechanism provides theoretical basis for increased alertness
> - No studies have found caffeine impairs reaction time in healthy adults"

> "I have chosen a **two-tailed** hypothesis because:
>
> - While some studies show music improves concentration (Rauscher et al., 1993), others show it impairs performance (Perham & Vizard, 2011)
> - The effect may depend on task type, music type, and individual differences
> - I cannot confidently predict which direction the effect will go"

**One-tailed version (if you can justify it):**

## Activity 3: Design & Analysis Matching (25 min)

### Choosing Your Design

**1. In a between-subjects design, participants:**

`r mcq(c(answer = "Experience only ONE condition each", "Experience ALL conditions", "Are matched in pairs across conditions", "Drop out before completing the study"))`

**2. In a within-subjects design, participants:**

`r mcq(c("Experience only one condition each", answer = "Experience ALL conditions", "Are tested only once", "Cannot be counterbalanced"))`

### Evaluating Trade-offs

**3. The main advantage of between-subjects designs is:**

`r mcq(c("More statistical power", "Fewer participants needed", answer = "No order effects, practice effects, or carryover", "Simpler randomisation"))`

**4. The main advantage of within-subjects designs is:**

`r mcq(c(answer = "Each participant serves as their own control, increasing power", "No need for counterbalancing", "Participants prefer them", "Faster data collection"))`

**5. The main disadvantage of within-subjects designs is:**

`r mcq(c("Less statistical power", "More participants needed", answer = "Order effects, practice effects, and demand characteristics", "Cannot use t-tests"))`

### Apply to Your Topic

**6. Describe what a between-subjects version of YOUR study would look like:**

> "Group A (n = ___): _________________________________________
>
> Group B (n = ___): _________________________________________"

**7. Describe what a within-subjects version of YOUR study would look like:**

> "All participants (n = ___) would: ____________________________
>
> Order would be counterbalanced by: __________________________"

**8. For YOUR topic, identify one specific trade-off:**

> "A key consideration for my study is: _________________________
>
> This makes [between / within] subjects more appropriate because: ___"

### Matching Design to Test

**9. A between-subjects design with two groups requires:**

`r mcq(c("Paired samples t-test", answer = "Independent samples t-test", "One-sample t-test", "Correlation"))`

**10. A within-subjects design with two conditions requires:**

`r mcq(c(answer = "Paired samples t-test", "Independent samples t-test", "One-sample t-test", "Chi-square test"))`

**11. Why does design determine the statistical test?**

`r mcq(c("Statistical software requires you to specify", "It's a tradition in psychology", answer = "Different designs produce different data structures that require different analyses", "Both tests give the same answer anyway"))`

### Write Your Complete Justification

> "This study will use a [between-subjects / within-subjects] design because:
>
> [Specific advantage for your topic]: _________________________
>
> [How you'll address the main disadvantage]: __________________
>
> Given this design, I will use a [independent / paired] samples t-test because [how the data structure matches the test]: _______________"
