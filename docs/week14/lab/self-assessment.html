Week 14 Self-Assessment

Reading Independent T-Test Output

Author

Gordon Wright

Published

4 February 2026

Overview

This self-assessment builds on your R output interpretation skills by focusing on independent samples t-tests - used when comparing two different groups of participants.

NoteFormative Assessment

This self-assessment is not graded but completing it will prepare you for: - The EDS Assignment (interpreting t-test output) - The Research Report (comparing groups)

Part 1: Reading Independent T-Test Output

Exercise 1: Basic Independent T-Test

A researcher compared anxiety scores between students who exercise regularly (n = 20) and those who don’t (n = 20). Here’s the R output:

> # Descriptive statistics
> data %>%
+   group_by(exercise) %>%
+   summarise(Mean = mean(anxiety), SD = sd(anxiety), n = n())

# A tibble: 2 x 4
  exercise       Mean    SD     n
  <chr>         <dbl> <dbl> <int>
1 no_exercise    48.2  12.4    20
2 regular        39.5  10.8    20

> # Independent t-test
> t.test(anxiety ~ exercise, data = data, paired = FALSE)

    Welch Two Sample t-test

data:  anxiety by exercise
t = 2.36, df = 37.2, p-value = 0.0236
alternative hypothesis: true difference in means is not equal to 0
95 percent confidence interval:
 1.21 16.19
sample estimates:
mean in group no_exercise   mean in group regular
                     48.2                    39.5

a) What type of t-test is this?

TipShow Answer

This is a Welch Two Sample t-test (independent samples t-test). It compares two different groups of participants.

b) What are the key statistics from this output?

TipShow Answer
  • t-statistic: t = 2.36
  • Degrees of freedom: df = 37.2 (Welch’s adjustment)
  • p-value: p = 0.0236
  • 95% CI for the difference: [1.21, 16.19]
  • Mean difference: 48.2 - 39.5 = 8.7 points

c) Is this result statistically significant at α = .05?

TipShow Answer

Yes, because p = .0236 < .05. We reject the null hypothesis.

d) Report this result in APA format.

TipShow Answer

An independent samples t-test revealed that students who did not exercise regularly reported significantly higher anxiety (M = 48.2, SD = 12.4) than those who exercised regularly (M = 39.5, SD = 10.8), t(37.2) = 2.36, p = .024, 95% CI [1.21, 16.19].

Exercise 2: Why Independent?

a) In Exercise 1, why was an independent t-test appropriate?

TipShow Answer

An independent t-test was appropriate because: - Two different groups of participants - Each person is in only one condition (exercise OR no exercise) - The groups are independent - scores in one group don’t affect scores in the other - This is a between-subjects design

b) What would make a paired t-test appropriate instead?

TipShow Answer

A paired t-test would be appropriate if: - The same participants were measured twice - For example: measuring anxiety BEFORE and AFTER an exercise program - Each data point in condition 1 is linked to a data point in condition 2 - This is a within-subjects design

Exercise 3: Interpreting the Confidence Interval

From Exercise 1:

95 percent confidence interval:
 1.21 16.19

a) What does this confidence interval represent?

TipShow Answer

We are 95% confident that the true population difference in anxiety scores between non-exercisers and regular exercisers is between 1.21 and 16.19 points.

b) Why does it matter that the interval doesn’t include zero?

TipShow Answer

If the interval included zero, the true difference might be zero (no real difference). Since it doesn’t include zero: - We can be confident there IS a real difference - This is consistent with the significant p-value - The groups genuinely differ in the population

Part 2: Design Recognition

Exercise 4: Independent or Paired?

For each study, identify whether an independent or paired t-test would be appropriate:

a) Comparing memory test scores between males and females.

TipShow Answer

Independent t-test - Different participants in each group (you can’t be both male and female in this study).

b) Measuring reaction time before and after drinking coffee.

TipShow Answer

Paired t-test - Same participants measured twice (before and after).

c) Comparing exam scores between students taught with Method A vs Method B.

TipShow Answer

Independent t-test - Different students in each teaching method (assuming random assignment to methods).

d) Rating the same 30 products for attractiveness and then for quality.

TipShow Answer

Paired t-test - Same products rated twice (on two different dimensions).

Exercise 5: Design Components

A study compares problem-solving ability between people who slept 8 hours vs 4 hours.

a) What is the independent variable?

TipShow Answer

Sleep duration (8 hours vs 4 hours) - the grouping variable.

b) What is the dependent variable?

TipShow Answer

Problem-solving ability (score on the problem-solving task).

c) What type of experimental design is this?

TipShow Answer

Between-subjects (independent groups) design - different people in each sleep condition.

d) What type of data is the DV?

TipShow Answer

Likely interval or ratio data (numerical scores on a problem-solving test).

Part 3: Reading and Reporting Practice

Exercise 6: Full Interpretation

A researcher investigated whether background music affects concentration. Participants completed a concentration task in silence (n = 25) or with background music (n = 25).

> data %>%
+   group_by(condition) %>%
+   summarise(Mean = mean(score), SD = sd(score), n = n())

# A tibble: 2 x 4
  condition  Mean    SD     n
  <chr>     <dbl> <dbl> <int>
1 music      72.4  11.2    25
2 silence    78.8   9.6    25

> t.test(score ~ condition, data = data)

    Welch Two Sample t-test

data:  score by condition
t = -2.17, df = 46.8, p-value = 0.0352
alternative hypothesis: true difference in means is not equal to 0
95 percent confidence interval:
 -12.33  -0.47
sample estimates:
mean in group music mean in group silence
               72.4                  78.8

a) State the hypothesis that this study was testing.

TipShow Answer

The researcher hypothesized that background music would affect concentration task performance. (More specifically, they may have predicted that music would either help or hinder concentration.)

b) What do the results show?

TipShow Answer

Participants who worked in silence scored higher (M = 78.8) than those with background music (M = 72.4). The difference of 6.4 points was statistically significant (p = .035).

c) Write a complete APA results paragraph.

TipShow Answer

An independent samples t-test was conducted to examine the effect of background music on concentration. Participants who completed the task in silence (M = 78.8, SD = 9.6) scored significantly higher than those who listened to background music (M = 72.4, SD = 11.2), t(46.8) = -2.17, p = .035, 95% CI [-12.33, -0.47]. These results suggest that background music may impair concentration on cognitive tasks.

Exercise 7: Non-Significant Results

A researcher compared creativity scores between morning people (n = 30) and evening people (n = 30).

> t.test(creativity ~ chronotype, data = data)

    Welch Two Sample t-test

data:  creativity by chronotype
t = 0.84, df = 57.1, p-value = 0.405
alternative hypothesis: true difference in means is not equal to 0
95 percent confidence interval:
 -2.81  6.89
sample estimates:
mean in group evening mean in group morning
                 52.3                  50.2

a) Is this result statistically significant?

TipShow Answer

No, the result is not significant because p = .405 > .05. We fail to reject the null hypothesis.

b) What does the confidence interval tell us?

TipShow Answer

The 95% CI [-2.81, 6.89] includes zero, which is consistent with a non-significant result. The true difference could be negative, zero, or positive - we can’t be confident about the direction of any difference.

c) How would you report this result?

TipShow Answer

An independent samples t-test revealed no significant difference in creativity scores between evening people (M = 52.3) and morning people (M = 50.2), t(57.1) = 0.84, p = .405, 95% CI [-2.81, 6.89].

Part 4: Conceptual Questions

Exercise 8: True or False

a) An independent t-test requires the same participants to be in both conditions.

TipShow Answer

False. An independent t-test compares different participants in each condition. If the same participants are in both conditions, you use a paired t-test.

b) If p > .05, we can conclude there is no difference between groups.

TipShow Answer

False. A non-significant result means we failed to find evidence of a difference. It doesn’t prove there is no difference - we may have lacked statistical power (e.g., small sample size).

c) The degrees of freedom in a Welch t-test can be a decimal number.

TipShow Answer

True. Welch’s correction adjusts the degrees of freedom based on the variances in each group, often resulting in non-integer df values.

d) A confidence interval that doesn’t include zero indicates a significant result.

TipShow Answer

True (at the corresponding alpha level). If the 95% CI doesn’t include zero, the p-value will be < .05.

Summary

After completing this self-assessment, you should be able to:

  • Read and interpret R independent t-test output
  • Extract t, df, p-value, and CI from t.test() output
  • Distinguish between independent and paired t-test situations
  • Report independent t-test results in APA format
  • Interpret confidence intervals for mean differences
