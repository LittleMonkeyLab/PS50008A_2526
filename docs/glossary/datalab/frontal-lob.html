Station 6: Frontal Lob

DataLab
Metacognition and calibration - self-prediction vs actual performance

Station Overview

The Frontal Lob station investigates metacognition — your ability to accurately predict your own performance. Before throwing ping pong balls at a target, participants rate their throwing ability. We then compare their self-rating to actual performance, calculating a calibration score that reveals whether people are overconfident, underconfident, or well-calibrated.

What Participants Do

  1. Before throwing: Rate your throwing ability (1–10)
  2. Stand at the marked line
  3. Throw 10 ping pong balls at the basket (dominant hand)
  4. Optionally: Throw 5 more with your non-dominant hand
  5. Count your successes

Research Questions

Is self-rated ability correlated with actual performance?

Are people generally overconfident or underconfident?

Does dominant vs non-dominant hand performance differ?

Experimental Design

Element Description
Design Correlational (self-rating vs performance)
Predictor Self-rated throwing ability (1–10)
Outcome Actual throwing success (0–10)
Measure Calibration = accuracy − rating

Data Variables

Variable Type Description Range
throw_self_rating Ordinal Self-rated ability (before) 1–10
throw_dom_success Count Dominant hand successes 0–10
throw_nondom_success Count Non-dominant hand successes 0–5
throw_dom_hand Categorical Dominant hand Left, Right

Calculated Variables

Variable Formula Interpretation
throw_accuracy_pct (dom_success / 10) × 100 Performance as percentage
throw_calibration accuracy_pct/10 - rating Calibration score
ImportantCalibration Score Interpretation
  • Positive calibration: Performed better than predicted → underconfident
  • Negative calibration: Predicted better than performed → overconfident
  • Zero calibration: Perfect self-knowledge!

Expected Results

Metric Typical Finding
Mean success rate ~40–60% (4–6 out of 10)
Self-rating mean Often slightly higher than actual
Calibration Slight overconfidence (negative mean)
Correlation (rating vs performance) Moderate positive (r ≈ 0.3–0.5)
NoteThe Dunning-Kruger Effect?

This task can reveal calibration biases. Research on metacognition shows that people are often poorly calibrated — we think we’re better at things than we actually are. However, the relationship is complex and depends on task difficulty and expertise.

Suggested Analyses

Primary analyses:

  • Pearson’s r: Correlation between self-rating and success
  • Scatterplot: Visualise the relationship

Calibration analysis:

  • Calculate mean calibration score
  • One-sample t-test: Is mean calibration different from 0?

Exploratory analyses:

  • Compare dominant vs non-dominant hand performance
  • Correlate conscientiousness (TIPI) with calibration

R Code Starter

# Load data
datalab <- read_csv("datalab_data.csv")

# Calculate calibration
datalab <- datalab |>
  mutate(
    throw_accuracy_pct = (throw_dom_success / 10) * 100,
    throw_calibration = (throw_accuracy_pct / 10) - throw_self_rating
  )

# Correlation between self-rating and performance
cor.test(datalab$throw_self_rating, datalab$throw_dom_success)

# Mean calibration (are people overconfident?)
datalab |>
  summarise(
    mean_calibration = mean(throw_calibration, na.rm = TRUE),
    sd_calibration = sd(throw_calibration, na.rm = TRUE)
  )

# One-sample t-test: Is calibration different from 0?
t.test(datalab$throw_calibration, mu = 0)

# Scatterplot
library(ggplot2)
ggplot(datalab, aes(x = throw_self_rating, y = throw_dom_success)) +
  geom_jitter(width = 0.2, height = 0.2, alpha = 0.6, colour = "#275882") +
  geom_smooth(method = "lm", se = TRUE, colour = "#EA8439") +
  geom_abline(slope = 1, intercept = 0, linetype = "dashed", colour = "grey50") +
  scale_x_continuous(breaks = 1:10) +
  scale_y_continuous(breaks = 0:10) +
  labs(
    x = "Self-Rated Ability (1-10)",
    y = "Actual Success (out of 10)",
    title = "Self-Prediction vs Actual Performance",
    subtitle = "Dashed line = perfect calibration"
  ) +
  coord_fixed()

Connection to Other Concepts

This station demonstrates:

  • Correlational Design — examining relationships between variables
  • Pearson’s r — measuring correlation strength
  • Scatterplot — visualising bivariate relationships

Tips for Facilitators

  • Ensure the throwing line is consistently positioned
  • Use standard ping pong balls and a consistent target
  • Record the dominant hand — this allows hand comparison
  • Encourage honest self-ratings before any practice throws
  • Non-dominant hand trials are optional but provide interesting comparisons

## Station Overview

Before throwing ping pong balls at a target, participants rate their throwing ability. We then compare their self-rating to actual performance, calculating a **calibration score** that reveals whether people are overconfident, underconfident, or well-calibrated.

> Are people generally overconfident or underconfident?
