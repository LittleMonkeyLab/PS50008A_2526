## Outcomes
C5, C13, C14, C16

## One-liner
From differences to relationships: scatterplots, correlation, and why correlation is not causation.

## Paragraph


## Structure
- 0-10 min: Shift from "is there a difference?" to "do these vary together?"
- 10-20 min: Scatterplots—direction, strength, form. Always plot first.
- 20-30 min: Pearson's r—what it measures, how to interpret strength
- 30-40 min: Correlation ≠ causation [C13]—examples, third variables, directionality
- 40-50 min: Pearson vs Spearman [C16], when to use which. No IV in correlational designs [C5].

## Key points for MCQ
- Correlation does not imply causation [C13]—must be able to articulate why
- Correlational design has "no IV" [C5]
- Pearson = parametric, Spearman = nonparametric [C16]
- Direction (positive/negative) vs strength (closer to ±1 = stronger)

## Examples and demos
- Data Olympics: confidence vs accuracy scatterplot
- Classic spurious correlations (ice cream and drowning, etc.)
- Show same r with very different scatterplots (Anscombe's quartet)

## Connections
- To lab: Run correlations in R, by-hand Pearson for EDS
- To project: Groups doing correlational designs identify their variables
- To prior weeks: Inference logic (week 14) applies to testing r ≠ 0
- To future weeks: Week 16 regression extends this; week 17 multiple comparisons

## Materials needed
- Scatterplot examples (good and pathological)
- Spurious correlations examples

## Pedagogy notes
Correlation ≠ causation is the big idea. Hammer it repeatedly with varied examples. Students often nod along but can't articulate *why* when asked. Make them practice explaining.
