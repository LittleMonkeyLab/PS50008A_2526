## Outcomes
C4, C8

## One-liner
Why the same measurement gives different answers: distributions, central tendency, spread, and levels of measurement.

## Paragraph


## Structure
- 0-5 min: Recap Data Olympics, show their data distributions (Room A vs Room B RT)
- 5-15 min: Central tendency—mean, median, mode. When each matters.
- 15-25 min: Spread—range, SD. What SD actually means visually.
- 25-35 min: Levels of measurement (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio) [MCQ requirement]
- 35-45 min: Sampling variability—"if we ran it again, what would change?"
- 45-50 min: Preview lab tasks

## Key points for MCQ
- Levels of measurement definitions and examples [C4]
- Mean vs median (skew sensitivity)
- SD as "typical distance from mean"

## Examples and demos
- Their RT distributions (histograms side by side)
- Classic skewed distribution (income) for mean vs median
- Animated sampling: "watch the mean jump around"

## Connections
- To lab: Calculate descriptives on their own data
- To project: Groups need to think about their DV measurement level
- To prior weeks: Week 11 data is the raw material
- To future weeks: Week 13 builds intervals around these estimates

## Pedagogy notes
Don't rush levels of measurement—it's dry but tested on MCQ. Use concrete examples for each level. The RT distributions should be a revelation: "look how different you all are."
