## Assessment 1: Research Plan (20%)

### Key Information
- **Deadline**: Friday 27 February 2026, 12 noon
- **Submission**: Via Moodle submission link on PS50008A VLE page
- **Word count**: Approximately 1,000 words
- **Credit value**: 6 credits
- **First signposted**: Week 14
- **Support in lecture**: Week 16

### Task

Describe how you would investigate ONE of the following research topics:

1. **The effect of caffeine on learning nouns.**

2. **The effect of TV programme content on the recall of advertisements presented part-way through the programme.**

3. **The effect of relaxation classes on stress reduction in the work place.**

### What to Include

Your answer should include:

**a) Experimental Hypothesis**
- State your experimental hypothesis clearly
- Note whether it is one-tailed or two-tailed
- Explain why you chose this direction

**b) Method Section**
A detailed Method section with the following subsections:

- **Design**: What type of design would you use (between-participants, within-participants, matched pairs)? Why is this design appropriate for your research question?

- **Participants**: Who would your participants be? How many? How would you recruit them? What are the inclusion/exclusion criteria?

- **Materials/Stimuli**: What materials would you need? Be specific about any stimuli, questionnaires, or equipment.

- **Procedure**: What would happen step by step? How would you control for extraneous variables? What instructions would participants receive?

**c) Draft Results Section**
- Draw a summary data table showing how you would present the data (with appropriate column headings—do NOT invent actual data)
- State which statistical test should be used to analyse the data
- Justify your choice of test (why this test and not another?)

### Important Notes
- Do NOT invent any data—this is a plan, not a completed study
- Focus on justifying your choices throughout
- Show that you understand the connection between design and analysis

### Marking Criteria
- Clear, testable hypothesis with appropriate direction
- Logical design choice with justification
- Sufficient procedural detail for replication
- Appropriate statistical test selection with rationale
- Professional presentation and clarity

### How This Assessment Prepares You
The Research Plan directly prepares you for:
- The MCQ Exam (week 18): Design identification, hypothesis types, test selection
- The Research Report (week 22): You will write a full Method section for your group project
- The EDS Assignment (week 20): Same skills in design and test selection

## Assessment 2: MCQ Exam (20%)

### Key Information
- **Date**: Monday 9 March 2026
- **Time**: During your normal lecture slot
- **Duration**: 1 hour
- **Location**: Normal lecture venue
- **Format**: Paper-based, multiple choice
- **Credit value**: 6 credits
- **First signposted**: Week 14
- **Dedicated prep**: Week 17 lecture

### Exam Structure

The paper contains multiple "Investigations"—each is a short scenario describing a research study. For each Investigation, you answer 6 questions testing the same core decisions:

1. **Design**: Identify the design (between-participants, within-participants, matched pairs, correlational)
2. **IV**: Identify the independent variable (or recognise there is no IV in correlational designs)
3. **DV**: Identify the dependent variable
4. **Hypothesis**: Identify or select the appropriate hypothesis (one-tailed or two-tailed)
5. **Test**: Select the appropriate statistical test
6. **Interpretation**: Interpret the p-value and decide whether results support the hypothesis

### What You Need to Know

**Design types:**
- Between-participants (independent groups)
- Within-participants (repeated measures)
- Matched pairs
- Correlational (no IV)

**Tests you must be able to select:**

| Design | Parametric | Non-parametric |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| Between-participants | Independent t-test | Mann-Whitney U |
| Within-participants | Paired t-test | Wilcoxon signed-rank |
| Correlational | Pearson's r | Spearman's rho |

**Key concepts:**
- One-tailed vs two-tailed hypotheses
- When to use parametric vs non-parametric tests
- What p-values mean (probability under null hypothesis)
- α = .05 significance threshold
- Correlation does not imply causation

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Selecting the right family but wrong test (e.g., independent t for repeated-measures data)
- Confusing correlation designs with experimental designs
- Getting hypothesis direction wrong
- Confusing IV with DV
- Category errors (confusing instrument with variable, participants with IV)

### How to Prepare
- Complete the weekly self-assessment MCQs throughout the term
- Review the MCQ decision framework: Design → Variables → Hypothesis → Test → Interpret
- Practice with past investigations
- Focus on the logic, not memorisation

### Learning Outcomes Assessed
- C1, C2, C3, C4: Design and variables
- C5, C6, C7: Hypotheses
- C10, C11, C12, C13: Inference
- C14, C15, C16: Test selection

## Assessment 3: EDS Home Assignment (20%)

### Key Information
- **Deadline**: Friday 20 March 2026, 12 noon
- **Submission**: Via Moodle submission link on PS50008A VLE page
- **Format**: Worked statistics assignment (not multiple choice)
- **Credit value**: 6 credits
- **Pass mark**: 50%
- **First signposted**: Week 17
- **Support in lecture**: Week 19

### Task Structure

You will receive a research scenario with actual raw data. You must:

**Section A: Statistical Mechanics**

1. **State the hypothesis**: Write the researcher's hypothesis in your own words

2. **Identify the design**:
   - What type of design is this?
   - What is the IV? (or state if no IV)
   - What is the DV?
   - What type of data is the DV? (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio)

3. **Calculate descriptives BY HAND**:
   - Calculate means for each condition
   - Calculate standard deviations for each condition
   - Show ALL your working (every step)

4. **Run appropriate test BY HAND**:
   - Choose the correct statistical test
   - Show ALL calculation steps
   - Report your test statistic and p-value

5. **Interpret results**:
   - Did the results support the hypothesis?
   - What can you conclude?

**Section B: Methods and Theory**

6. **Methodological improvements**: Suggest TWO ways the study could be improved methodologically (with justification)

7. **Individual differences**: Discuss how individual differences might affect the results, or suggest an extension to the study

8. **Ecological validity**: Propose a more ecologically valid alternative design (minimum half a page)

### Critical Requirements

**You MUST show your working:**
- Full marks require choosing the right test AND showing all calculation steps
- Using R/software only = partial credit (10-12/20 maximum)
- Showing only final results = minimal credit (5-6/20)
- Wrong test family = zero for that section

**Tests you must be able to compute by hand:**
- Mean and standard deviation
- Paired-samples t-test OR Wilcoxon signed-rank test
- Pearson correlation coefficient

### Marking Breakdown
- Section A (statistical mechanics): Approximately 60%
- Section B (methods and theory): Approximately 40%
- More detail in Section B = more marks

### How to Prepare
- Practice by-hand calculations in labs (weeks 14-15)
- Use the EDS practice paper
- Understand the logic, not just the formula
- Practice writing methodological critiques

### Learning Outcomes Assessed
- C1-C4: Design identification
- C5, C6, C7: Hypotheses
- C10, C11, C12: Inference and interpretation
- P1: By-hand descriptives
- P4 or P5: By-hand inferential test
- R6, R7: Methods critique and ecological validity
- P10: APA reporting

## Assessment 4: Research Report (40%)

### Key Information
- **Deadline**: Friday 1 May 2026, 12 noon
- **Submission**: Via Moodle submission link on PS50008A VLE page
- **Word count**: 2,500 words (±10%)
- **Credit value**: 12 credits
- **Format**: Individual lab report in APA format
- **First signposted**: Week 16
- **Support in lectures**: Weeks 19-20

### Task

Write an individual research report based on your group project data (or an alternative dataset if provided).

### Report Structure

Your report should follow standard APA lab report format:

**Title Page**
- Informative title
- Your name and student number

**Abstract** (approximately 150 words)
- Brief summary of rationale, method, results, and conclusions

**Introduction**
- Background and rationale for the study
- Review of relevant literature
- Clear statement of research question
- Hypothesis (with justification for direction)

**Method**
- Design (with justification)
- Participants (demographics, recruitment, ethics)
- Materials (all stimuli, questionnaires, equipment)
- Procedure (step-by-step, sufficient for replication)

**Results**
- Descriptive statistics (M, SD, presented in table or figure)
- Assumption checking (where appropriate)
- Inferential test results in APA format
- Effect size and confidence intervals
- Clear statement of whether hypothesis was supported

**Discussion**
- Interpretation of results
- Link back to introduction and literature
- Limitations of the study
- Suggestions for future research
- Conclusions

**References**
- APA format
- All sources cited in text must appear in references

### Key Requirements

**Statistical reporting in APA format:**
- t-test: *t*(df) = X.XX, *p* = .XXX, *d* = X.XX
- Correlation: *r*(df) = .XX, *p* = .XXX
- Include 95% confidence intervals where appropriate

**What we're looking for:**
- Clear, logical structure
- Appropriate use of literature
- Correct statistical analysis
- Accurate APA formatting
- Critical interpretation (not just description)
- Acknowledgment of limitations
- Appropriate causal language (especially for correlational designs)

### Report Writing Support Throughout Term

The module builds your report-writing skills progressively:

| Weeks | Focus | Report Section |
|-------|-------|----------------|
| 12-15 | Results reporting | How to report descriptives, CIs, effect sizes, test results |
| 16-17 | Methods & Introduction | How to write Method; how to build a rationale |
| 18-19 | Continue Intro | Hypothesis statements, literature review basics |
| 20 | Discussion | How to interpret, link back, acknowledge limitations |

## Key Differences: MCQ vs EDS

| Aspect | MCQ Exam | EDS Assignment |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Format | Multiple choice | Worked problems, show all steps |
| Timing | 1 hour, in lecture slot | Take-home, 1 week |
| Calculation | None (interpretation only) | By hand, show ALL steps |
| Test selection | Recognise correct test | Choose AND compute |
| Hypothesis | Select from options | Write in own words |
| Methods critique | Not assessed | Required (Section B) |
| Software | Not assessed | Optional (partial credit only) |
| p-value | Interpret given value | Calculate yourself |

## Academic Integrity

All assessments must be your own work:
- **Research Plan**: Individual submission
- **MCQ Exam**: Closed-book, no collaboration
- **EDS Assignment**: Individual submission, no collaboration on calculations
- **Research Report**: Individual write-up (even though data comes from group project)

Using AI tools to generate your work is not permitted. You may use AI to help understand concepts, but submitted work must be your own.
