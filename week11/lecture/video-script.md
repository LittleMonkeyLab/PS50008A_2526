## SLIDE 3: Why Research Methods?

**[Show: Why research methods slide]**

Psychology is a science. That means we don't just have opinions about human behaviour — we test those opinions systematically.

To make credible claims about how people think, feel, and behave, we need:
- Systematic observation
- Controlled experiments
- Statistical analysis
- And peer review

This module gives you the tools to do all of this.

## SLIDE 6: What Is DataLab?

**[Show: DataLab description slide]**

DataLab is a series of six cognitive task stations.

You'll test your reaction times, complete memory tasks, make estimations, and rate your confidence.

You'll work in pairs — taking turns as participant and tester. And you'll record everything in Qualtrics using your phone or laptop.

---

## SLIDE 7: Why DataLab?

**[Show: Table showing how student data maps to topics]**

Why do we call it DataLab? Because the data YOU generate becomes our teaching dataset for the entire term.

When we learn about distributions, we'll look at YOUR reaction times.
When we learn about t-tests, we'll compare Room A versus Room B.
When we learn about correlations, we'll explore YOUR memory and confidence scores.

You'll learn statistics using YOUR own data — not abstract textbook examples.

---

## SLIDE 8: The 6 Stations (First Three)

**[Show: Stations 1-3 slide]**

Let me quickly walk you through the six stations.

Station 1 is Simple Reaction Time — how fast can you respond when a stimulus appears?

Station 2 is Choice Reaction Time — what happens when you have to make a decision about which button to press?

Station 3 is a Memory Task — how many items can you hold in working memory?

---

## SLIDE 9: The 6 Stations (Last Three)

**[Show: Stations 4-6 slide]**

Station 4 is an Estimation Task — how accurate are your numerical judgments?

Station 5 is Group Estimation — does discussing with others improve your estimates?

And Station 6 is Confidence Ratings — how well do you know what you know?
